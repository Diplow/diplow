    Chat

    The left of the screen, cut along a curve: your words and the agent's, and everything the conversation asks of you or shows you beyond words. Its components share one column, 460px of text from 36px in, and their zones run edge to edge, to the curve.

    widgets

    The widget

    Everything the conversation asks of you or shows you beyond words is a widget: a zone in the flow of messages, tinted by whose it is. One shape for all of them, so you learn it once: an icon, a kicker, a title and a subtitle, a body, its actions, and its tools when you reach for them.

    anatomy

    • The zone: a solid surface the width of the conversation, one hairline on top, tinted toward the curve by its voice. Violet when the agent asks, amber when it holds your words, none when it is the app's (a key, help).
    • The icon: a hex the size of a small tile, a line icon inside. A widget about a tile shows the tile's place instead, a small map of its frame.
    • The kicker: who, or what state, in mono, in the voice. The title, in Newsreader: what it is about. The subtitle, one line of Geist.
    • The body: whatever the widget is for, a list, a form, a key. The actions: text buttons; the first is the one it expects, in its voice, with its shortcut.
    • The tools show on hover or focus only: settings, fold, dismiss.

    states

    • Working: the hex breathes and light runs over the lines to come.
    • Done: the zone gives its surface back and leaves one line, the receipt, with an undo while one is possible.
    • Failed: the voice turns destructive, the widget stays with a retry.

    motion

    Its top line draws itself left to right, the surface unfolds from it, then its parts rise one after another (--stagger-bloom apart). It folds back the same way. With reduced motion, it is simply there.

    A widget waiting for you that scrolls out of sight: mark it on the curve, a small light in its voice that brings it back on click?

    The tile widget

    Creating or editing a tile happens in the conversation, as a widget whose title is the tile's title. It is amber while the words are yours, violet while they are the agent's draft; take the draft to edit it and the zone warms from one to the other.

    Add a tile for my tooling, west of Projects.

    anatomy

    • The icon is the place: the frame the tile goes in, its slot lit. Creating, the free slots are dashed and a click moves the tile there; the canvas shows the draft in that slot, breathing in the widget's voice.
    • The title is the field, set in Newsreader at the widget's title size, underlined only when you are in it. Then the kind (a branch can hold tiles, a leaf can't, a Context tile says what its parent is), the preview with its count, and the page.
    • The count shows from 280 of 350, counted as a reader counts (an emoji is one), ember near the end, destructive past it.
    • The page is written in Newsreader, as your words are. Write and Preview switch in place; the expand tool gives it the canvas's side of the screen.

    states

    • Edit marks each changed field with a small dot; Save sends only those.
    • The agent's edit shows what it changes in place: struck in faint, added on a violet ground. Accept, edit it, or discard.
    • Changed meanwhile: the tile's version moved while you typed. Your words stay; the widget says who changed what, and lets you compare.

    Today the forms open in a Drawer (HEX-79). Moving them into the conversation is the change this proposes.

    The key widget

    How an agent outside hexframe reaches your system. The app's own widget, so it carries no voice: a name, a key shown once beside the command that uses it, and the keys you already gave.

    /keys

    anatomy

    • First key: one sentence on what a key gives (your full access, through MCP), a name of 1 to 32 characters, and the long-hex button.
    • Shown once: the key inks in from a blur, on a faint ground, with its copy; under it the command, ready to paste, the key inside it. One ember line says it won't be shown again. Copying sweeps a light over the block and the label turns to Copied.
    • Your keys: one row each, a name, then in faint mono how it starts (hf_ and 3), when it was made, when last used. A key used in the last hour has a small green light. Revoke shows on hover and asks first (the dialog, 1.7).

    behaviour

    • Done, or leaving, forgets the key; the widget folds to its receipt, "Key issued: Claude Code, laptop", never the key itself.
    • Managing keys needs your session: a widget the agent opened shows them but can't issue one.

    The help widget

    Help is a system of tiles shipped with the app. In the conversation it opens one of them where you are, rather than sending you to another page: its place among the six, its page, and where to read on.

    How do directions work?

    anatomy

    • The icon is Help's own frame: its six topics around it, the one you read lit. A click on another slot turns the page, so the map is also the way around.
    • The page in Geist at reading size, its headings in Newsreader, its tables and diagrams drawn as the canvas draws (the six directions as a frame, not a table).
    • Read on: the tiles it names, as mentions, and "Open in Help", which centers the canvas on Help at this tile.

    three ways in

    • Asking: the agent answers and opens the topic it read from.
    • /help and a few letters: the topics that match, the words found in them marked.
    • ? anywhere: the shortcuts, grouped by where they work.

    The shortcuts shown are proposals: TanStack Hotkeys is chosen in STACK but not installed yet.

    The dialog

    The one thing that stops you. Kept for what can't be undone, or loses work. It opens over the conversation's column, not the centre of the screen, so the canvas stays in view and shows what the action touches.

    Delete Site, I'll never build it.

    Site has two tiles below it. Deleting it takes them too.

    asks before deleting
    Delete Site
    And the 2 tiles below it.

    anatomy

    • A risen surface, square, a line on top in its voice that draws itself as it opens: destructive for what is lost, neutral otherwise.
    • A kicker naming the act, a title asking it as a question about the thing itself, "Delete Site?", and one paragraph of consequence, counted.
    • What it touches, drawn: the frame, the tiles that go lit in the destructive colour. The canvas behind lights the same ones.
    • Two actions: a quiet way out first, then the act, named with its count ("Delete 3 tiles"), as the long-hex button.

    behaviour

    • Focus starts on the way out. esc leaves, ↵ confirms only from the button.
    • A branch of more than one level asks for its name to be typed.
    • A click on the dim behind leaves, but not once something is typed.
    • Revoking a key, discarding an edit: the same dialog, neutral or destructive by what is lost.

    When the agent asks in the conversation (the widget on the left), is the dialog still needed? I'd say yes for a branch: the typed name is the safety.

    2

    Canvas

    The rest of the screen: your system as tiles of light, a center and its six. A tile is drawn by its light alone; what it is, where it stands, what happens to it and who touches it change that light, and never add a box.

    tiles

    What a tile can be

    A tile's light says what it is before its words do. A Branch's light fades toward its foot, where its frame shows in small; a Leaf's is even, a page with nothing below; the Context is teal; the root is warm, since it is you.

    Hover a tile for its card, or tab to it

    light, not lines

    • A Branch fills with tile fading to tile-fade, top to foot: there is more below. At its foot, its frame in small, a dot where each child is.
    • A Leaf is lit evenly, halfway between the two: a page, nothing below, no frame at its foot. A file that isn't Markdown shows its name in mono.
    • The root is the only warm tile, glow fading to glow-soft, with its halo behind. Warm is yours.

    the Context

    • A Context tile takes the Context's teal as a light and one hairline, the only tile with a line of its own but the center.
    • A Reference shows the tile it points at, its hairline dashed, since the tile lives elsewhere, and where, under its title. Broken, its place stays, faint, and says so; it never blocked the delete.

    nothing yet, and strain

    • A free slot shows a plus only where a click adds a tile. Where nothing can be added (Help, a read-only view), the place is dotted, with no plus.
    • An empty system: the root untitled, its rim dashed, waiting for a name or a vault.
    • Ember (warning) marks what the shape can't seat as asked: a Leaf numbered as a Branch already is, a ring of more than six shown as a list.

    The frame at a Branch's foot is new: the app tells a Leaf by a muted fill today. Is it enough, with the even light, to tell them apart at a glance?

    How a tile answers

    A tile answers your hand, its place in the frame, and time, each by its light: lit under the pointer, lined at the center, dimmed away from it, blooming out of its parent and folding back.

    The last row replays on click

    to your hand

    • Hovered, the light rises to tile-lit; after a moment, the card: its place, its title, its preview in full.
    • Focused from the keyboard, the ring's amber line, 1.5px. Pressed, a hair smaller while held.
    • Its menu open, it stays lit, with a line; the others step back to half. Not offered, during a move or a pick, a third of its light, and no hover.

    by its place

    • The center is lit and keeps the only steady line, its title in italics. The root at the center warms its line toward amber.
    • A centered Leaf has no ring to show: it fills the canvas alone, and takes no click.
    • Away from the frame, half its light beside it, its title leaning in so it stays on screen; further out, a trace, 13%.

    over time

    • Tiles bloom out of their parent's heart (800ms, 40ms apart) and fold back into it (500ms). "Show where" raises an amber glow and lets it fade.
    • A write shows at once. Refused, a destructive glow, and the tile goes back to how it was; why, in a toast or on the form's field.
    • Loading, light sweeps over where the tiles will be, no spinner.
    Click a Branch of the ring, or the center

    in a frame

    • A Branch of the ring opens in place: its light gives way to a faint ground, the Branch at its heart and its six blooming out of it. A click on its heart folds them back.
    • The center shows its Context inside it, on a teal ground: its own Context tiles and the References, dashed. A click on the center again hides it.
    • Anything else, a click travels to it: the camera, drawn in the prototype, not here.

    The opened rings are drawn small here. On a real screen the center is about three times this size, and their titles read at 13px or more.

    Text in a tile

    A tile's words grow with it, then stop. Its title is laid out at 16px and scaled with the tile, never above 22px; far away it shows alone, never below 11px. Its preview comes when there is room, as many lines as the hex holds. Drag the size, or write your own.

    who acts

    Who touches a tile

    The canvas shows who is acting on it, in their colour: amber where you point, violet where the agent reads, works, drafts or proposes, a hollow light for a key from outside. What happened while you looked away stays as a small dot until you have seen it.

    you, in amber

    • You point at it: the tile you hover in the conversation, or are on in the @ list, glows amber and holds it. "Show where" is the same light, rising once.
    • Your own change pings amber once, and leaves no dot: you saw it.

    the agent, in violet

    • Reads: a violet light passes over each tile it opens, as the events list them.
    • Works here: when a message names a tile first (HEX-84), the agent's light runs around that tile's rim while its turn lasts.
    • Drafts: the tile it is writing, in its light, breathing (animate-breathe), its title in mono.
    • Proposes, asking first (HEX-85): what it would do, not done yet. A new tile as a dashed violet outline, a move as an arc to where it would go, an edit as the tile's rim, dashed violet: dashed violet is what isn't done yet. Accepting in the conversation lands them.
    • Changed it, applying with undo: a violet ping, then a violet dot until you open the tile.

    a key, hollow

    • An agent outside, through a Key: a hollow ping and a hollow dot. Its card names the key and when: "Claude Code, laptop edited it, a minute ago".

    None of these marks is planned yet. HEX-83 gives the live step; whether the canvas follows it is the question.

    acting on a tile

    The tile’s menu

    Everything you can do to a tile, where the tile is: a right-click, a long press, or ⇧F10 on a focused tile. Each line has its key, so the menu teaches the keys. Where the action is a place, the submenu is the frame itself.

    Right-click a tile, or an empty slot

    anatomy

    • A header: the tile's title in Newsreader and, in faint mono, its kind and place, so you know what you act on. The tile stays lit; the others step back.
    • Groups, by distance: going there (open, go in, its Context), changing it (edit, branch or leaf), placing it (add a tile, move, swap), the agent, sharing (copy, export), then delete, alone at the end in the destructive colour.
    • Its keys on the right, in faint mono. Typing one with the menu open runs it.
    • A submenu that is a place (add a tile, move) shows the frame: free slots dashed, a taken slot offers a swap, the line under it says what the click will do.
    • Ask the agent is violet: it puts the tile in the composer, named first, so the agent works there.

    motion

    It opens from the pointer, growing a little (140ms); a submenu opens beside it on hover or →, never covering the tile.

    Today the actions sit on a card beside the canvas and Move is a mode. A menu with the frame as its submenu could retire the mode.

    Moving a tile

    A tile goes where you put it: drag it, or choose Move in its menu or press M. While it moves, each free slot offers itself and each Branch offers to trade places; the rest steps back.

    or drag any tile

    while it moves

    • The tile on the move lifts: a little larger, its rim a dashed amber line that runs. Dragged, it follows the pointer.
    • A free slot warms under the pointer, dashed amber, and says where: "Here". Any free slot of the frame takes either kind.
    • A Branch offers to trade places at its foot, a small hex with two arrows, apart from its own click, which still opens it.
    • The rest takes no part: the parent, a Leaf, a Reference, anything above or below the moving tile. A third of its light, no hover.
    • A banner in your amber across the top: what moves, and esc to stop.

    landing

    • The tile glides to its slot (600ms, ease-glide) and the write is sent as it lands. Refused, it glides back with a destructive glow, and the move resumes so another slot can be picked.

    The app moves by a mode from the card, with no drag. Drag is proposed here, with the menu's frame as the keyboard's way.

    where you are

    The path

    Top right, where you are: one thin line from the root down to the tile you're on, a point per step, with the theme's disc at its head. The disc and the points are threaded on the line, so the corner reads as one mark rather than a button beside a list.

      anatomy

      • The disc, the theme: half lit, 21px in a 31px target, turning half a turn as the light changes. The line starts at the foot of its rim.
      • One line, 1px of input, fading out below the last point.
      • A point per step, hollow with a faint rim; the last, the tile you're on, lit amber with its glow.
      • A label to the left of each point, 14px from it, Geist 300 at 13px, faint; the current one in the foreground.

      the axis

      • Every part is centred on the line, none placed from the screen's edge on its own: a 31px column, the line at its centre, the disc and the points centred in it. Placed apart, they drift: the disc sat 3.5px off the line, the points 1px.
      • The sizes are odd (31, 21, 7, rows of 17), so each centre falls on a half pixel, where a 1px line's centre is. The line stays one crisp pixel and no circle straddles it.

      behaviour

      • A label goes back up to that tile; the current one is aria-current="location". The disc's aria-pressed says dark.
      • Below 820px the line and its points go, and the disc stays alone in the corner.
