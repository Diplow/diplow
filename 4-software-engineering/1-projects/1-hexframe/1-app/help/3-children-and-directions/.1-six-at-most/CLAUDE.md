  Why a Tile has six Children at most, and what to do when a seventh asks for
  room: regroup some Children under a new Tile, by moving them. The limit is
  the exercise, not an obstacle to work around.
---
Six is few on purpose. A reader can hold six things at once and compare them; past that, they start skimming, and the order you chose stops meaning anything.

So when a seventh Child asks for room, hexframe refuses it. The way forward is to regroup: create a new Tile that names what some of the Children have in common, move them under it, and the Directions they free take the rest.

That regrouping is the exercise. Finding the name for what two or three Children share is often the most useful thing you learn about your system.
