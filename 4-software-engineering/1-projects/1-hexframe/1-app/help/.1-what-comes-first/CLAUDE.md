  The idea behind hexframe: a reader takes what you show first as what matters
  most. Choosing the six Children of a Tile, and their order, is the work; the
  hierarchy it leaves behind is your intent, written down where an agent can
  follow it.
---
Anyone reading a system starts somewhere. Left alone, they start wherever they land: the first file, the loudest meeting, the latest message. Hexframe makes you choose where they start.

Each Tile shows six Children at most, so you choose which six, and in which Directions. That choice is the work. It is also hard on purpose: when a seventh thing asks for room, two of the six probably belong together under a new Tile.

What you get is a hierarchy a reader can trust. Opening a Tile, they see the six things that matter most about it, each with a Preview short enough to decide whether to go further. An agent reading it that way spends its attention where you would have.
