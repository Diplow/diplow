You maintain a system: a codebase, a team, your own life. Hexframe lets you lay it out as a hierarchy of Tiles. One Tile says what the whole is; the six below it say what it breaks into; each of those breaks down again. What you put first is what matters most, and that order carries your intent.

A reader, human or agent, reads it the same way: one Tile, then the Previews of its Children, then only the Children worth opening. An agent that reads your System this way works along what you meant, not along whatever it found first.

This help is a hexframe too, a System shipped with the app. Every user reads it and nobody writes it. Its Children:

1. **A System**: what you own, and why its Root is you.
2. **Tiles**: a Title, a Preview and a Body.
3. **Children and Directions**: the six places below a Tile.
4. **Context**: what a Tile is, beside what it does, and References.
5. **Operations**: create, edit, move, swap, delete.
6. **Keys and the MCP**: how an agent reaches your System.

Its Context holds the one idea behind all of it: what comes first matters most.
