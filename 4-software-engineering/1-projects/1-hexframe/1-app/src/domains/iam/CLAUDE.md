# iam

Identity and access: who someone is, and what they may do. Someone signs up with an email and a password and becomes an **Account**; each time they sign in, on a device, they hold a **Session** there until it expires or they sign out. Everything else in hexframe asks IAM one question first: is this request signed in, and as whom? A page or a server function that only a signed-in Account may reach starts from `signedIn`, which answers the proven Account and the proof that gave it, or fails `SignedOut`: a server function's from the Session its cookie proves, an MCP call's from its Key, `/mcp` being the one door a Key opens, and the Turn's sandbox's two doors the others a Turn's Key opens ([[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/1-app/src/api/CLAUDE|api]]). The client sends a `SignedOut` to home, signed out, which signs in at the chat's foot and goes back. Managing Keys, changing the Account itself, starting a Turn and changing a Tile's Grants (HEX-128) start from IAM's Session-only check instead, and so will, with sharing, managing an Organization, its members, Groups and join requests, never from a branch in the API layer. Then IAM answers a second question when asked: may this Account do this, its **Entitlement**.

The language, as [[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/STACK|STACK]] first told it:

- **Account**: someone known to hexframe. Its name is not IAM's to decide: the user is their Root tile in Mapping, and the name Better Auth keeps for emails is copied from that Tile's Title, never the other way. Until Mapping does, it is empty.
- **Session**: an Account's proven presence, for a while, on one device.
- **Key**: a credential an Account issues to a program (an MCP client, a script) and can revoke. It carries a name the Account gives it, acts with the Account's full power over its System, and never expires; its secret is shown once, at its creation, and never again. Revoking it removes it. With sharing, it acts with its Account's rights in every System, as the Account would; limiting a Key to one Tile or to reading comes after sharing, and OAuth clients come beside Keys later, without replacing them.
- **Turn's Key**: a credential IAM issues when a Session starts a Turn of the Assistant, bound to its Account, and to the Turn it was issued for, by the Turn's id, which IAM keeps and hands back without reading, the one secret the Turn's sandbox holds. It opens only `/mcp` and the Assistant's two sandbox doors, never a server function, which never reads a Bearer header, so it manages no Key and starts no Turn; it proves nothing past the Turn's maximum length, and the app revokes it when the Turn ends, needing no Session. It is no Key of the Account's: never listed among them, never named in the timeline, where its writes read as the Assistant's. With sharing, it is bound to the System its Turn started in as well: it reads and writes there alone, with its Account's rights, so a Turn started on someone else's Tile can neither carry their content into its Account's own Systems nor be turned against them, and a Turn started at home never reaches a System shared with its Account. That System is the Turn's, which Assistant keeps (`elsewhere`), and the API layer reads it from the Turn on every call the Key makes, never from a copy (HEX-134): IAM proves the Key, its Account and its Turn, and keeps nothing more. Its secret, `hft_` and 43 characters, is handed once, redacted, to the code that starts the Turn.
- **Signed in**: a request whose Account is proven, by a Session, a Key or a Turn's Key. Working on the System asks only that, never which proof it was. Managing Keys, changing the Account itself (email, password, billing), starting a Turn and changing a Tile's Grants, or reading who reaches a Tile, take a Session, and so will, with sharing, managing an Organization, its members, Groups and join requests, so a leaked Key cannot keep itself alive, nor a Turn start another, nor either open a System to Everyone.
- **Entitlement**: something an Account may do, derived when asked. One so far, `assistant`, may this Account use the Assistant: until Stripe, an Account has it when `ASSISTANT_ACCOUNTS`, a list of emails kept by hand, names its email, listed once its Account exists since no email is verified ([[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/1-app/src/repositories/auth/CLAUDE|auth]], `allowlist.ts`); then it is derived from what the Account pays for, so it never drifts from Stripe. How much it may spend is a setting of the Assistant until credits exist, not IAM's.

"Turn's Key" is IAM's word, a kind of proof with rules of its own, which doors it opens and that it is no Session; the Turn it names is Assistant's, which IAM holds by its id and never reads (`hexframe-app-assistant/decisions.md#DEC-19`). Better Auth, the allowlist, and later Stripe's plugin are repositories below IAM ([[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/1-app/src/repositories/auth/CLAUDE|auth]]); the plugin will own the subscription tables and the Stripe webhook. A Turn's Key lives in IAM's own table, `turn_key`, through the turn-keys repository ([[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/1-app/src/repositories/database/CLAUDE|database]]), which Better Auth does not keep: why is `hexframe-app-assistant/decisions.md#DEC-11`. No domain says "billing". AI usage is what a paid Entitlement buys; the structure itself stays free.

With sharing ("hexframe app: Sharing & organizations" in Linear, as its grill of 2026-10-17 settled it), IAM learns who belongs where, Organizations and their members since HEX-136, their Groups since HEX-137, join requests after; what each may do on a Tile stays Mapping's, which the API layer hands, on every request and every MCP call of a Turn, never from a copy kept in a Session or a Turn, the owner ids an Account stands for (its own, and the Organizations it is an admin of) and the Groups it belongs to:

- **Organization**: a team that owns a System of its own, whose star is the Organization, so its work belongs to no one member. Any Account creates one and is its first admin. Its name and Handle are its star's, Mapping's: IAM keeps an Organization by its id alone, which owns its System's rows as an Account's id owns its own, and never a copy of its name (`hexframe-app-sharing-organizations/decisions.md#DEC-13`). It keeps no timeline: a write in its System is told to the writer alone, offering no Undo.
- **Member**: an Account in an Organization, an **admin** or a **member**, and whether it joined of its own accord, as its maker did, rather than being added. IAM answers who is an admin; what being one lets them do in the Organization's System is Mapping's, which counts its admins as its owner. Admins manage its members and Groups; the last admin who joined can neither leave nor be demoted, while an admin only added may always leave. An admin adds someone without asking first; that they are told comes with HEX-138. Anyone may leave, an admin taking out themselves or a member leaving from the Members widget; leaving, being removed, being taken out of a Group, being demoted and a Group's deletion all hold from the next request on, since nothing is cached.
- **Group**: a set of an Organization's members, named once within it, whatever the case, 1 to 40 characters as a reader counts them, never nested, seen with who is in it by every member, so they know who they share with, changed by admins only, from a Session; leaving the Organization leaves its Groups. Every Organization has its **Members** Group, which holds them all and is kept by no one: its id is the Organization's own (`membersGroupOf`), so it needs no row, and a Grant naming an Organization's star is for it. A Group of its own has an id IAM makes, a UUID, which a Grant names, a Group having no star, and only a member of its Organization names it so. A deleted Group's id is never handed to Mapping again, so its Grants resolve for nobody; nothing writes into the Systems that named it, and the Share widget says a Grant set to it gone, for its owner to clear (`hexframe-app-sharing-organizations/decisions.md#DEC-14`).
- **Join request**: an Account asking to join an Organization, or a member one of its Groups, open until an admin accepts or declines it; one open at most per Account and Group.

## Rules

- **A refusal names its field, never the server's sentence.** Better Auth's refusals become IAM's errors here, each on the form field the user can fix; the message table words them. A wrong password and an unknown email are the same `CredentialsRejected`, on the `password` field in both cases, so sign-in never says which Accounts exist.
- **The Account comes from the request, not the input.** A server function acting for an Account takes it from `signedIn` (or `sessionOnly`), never an id the caller sends.
- **Two slots.** `CurrentSession` holds what a cookie proves, `CurrentKey` what a Bearer header proves, a Key or a Turn's Key; a server function's `CurrentKey` is always none, `/mcp`'s `CurrentSession` too. `signedIn` takes either, the Session first, and answers `{ account, by }`, `by` the proof it took, so the API layer can say who acted on the bus's envelope while a caller that needs only the Account reads `account`; `sessionOnly` takes the Session and answers a Key or a Turn's Key alone with `SessionRequired`, so `issueKey`, `keys`, `revokeKey` and `issueTurnKey` start from it, and so will any change to the Account itself.
- **A Turn's Key is read by its shape.** A Bearer secret starting `hft_` is looked up as a Turn's Key alone, by its hash, unexpired by IAM's clock; any other goes to Better Auth as an Account's Key. Its secret leaves IAM once, redacted, in `issueTurnKey`'s answer, and only its SHA-256 is kept.
- **An Entitlement is asked for, never stored.** `entitledOnly` refuses with `EntitlementMissing` (Forbidden) where the API layer needs it, before a Turn starts; `entitled` answers the same question for a page to say so.
- **A Key's name is IAM's.** `issueKey` trims a name and refuses one outside 1 to 32 characters with `KeyNameInvalid` before Better Auth sees it, counting as a reader counts, an emoji one, as the key widget counts it (`entities/names.ts`). The rule is IAM's alone: the plugin keeps only `requireName`, its bound on a name's length, in UTF-16 units, lifted.
- **A Key's secret is shown once.** `issueKey` answers it; Better Auth keeps only its hash, and `keys` lists a Key by its name, its first characters (`start`, `hf_` and three more), its creation and its last use.
- **Email and password only, for now.** Another way in (a social provider, a magic link) is a decision, and the first to need a callback URL will register it on each host Better Auth answers on ([[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/1-app/src/repositories/auth/CLAUDE|auth]]).
