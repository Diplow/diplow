# ui

hexframe's design system: the look, the words for its screen, and the closed list of components I own. The look was found in a prototype, then written down as tokens and fonts, which `src/front/styles.css` holds now; what follows is its direction and the words for its screen.

## The direction

hexframe's why is in [[4-software-engineering/1-projects/1-hexframe/CLAUDE|hexframe]]: agents now do more than we can follow, so an agent **unfolds** its work on a hierarchy of tiles, goal first, and the user keeps up with it at the **altitude** they choose. The look follows: going down a level is getting closer, the screen a universe explored one altitude at a time.

Intelligence has become common; experience has not. A person stands at the crossroads of the things they care about, and hexframe bets that this care deserves their best: to deepen what they understand of each, lay it out, what matters most first, and share it, with the world and with the agents that work beside them. In hexframe's universe each person is a sun: what they understand turns around them, and the agents work by its light. The look is futuristic because hexframe is a bet on a future worth wanting: human experience at the center, people directing agents that help them find where they want to explore. Futuristic here means space, imbalance, fine lines and light, not chrome. Obsidian is the closest relative. `/design-system` opens on this, in words, before any screen.

1. **Empty space and imbalance.** Nothing is centered for its own sake. On hexframe's main screen the canvas takes about 60% of the width, off to the right, and the tiles around the center run on behind the conversation instead of stopping at a frame. Space is the luxury: a screen is never filled to show it is busy.
2. **Light before lines.** A surface is drawn by its light, not by an outline: a tile is a fill, lighter than the ground, with the gap between tiles showing the ground. An opened tile loses its outline and leaves its own tile and its six around it. Only the center keeps a line. Where a separator is needed it is a hairline or a curve, never a box: a zone of the conversation is a solid fill with one fine line on top, a field is one underline.
3. **One warm light, at the root.** Amber belongs to the human. The root of a system, your own tile, holds its one warm light: lit amber, with a glow behind it that stays there as you move. An Organization's star is no person: it glows rose instead (`organization`), hexframe's own among them, which Help and the tutorial stand under, so amber still says "you" and rose says "us"; whatever the star, the corner's point where you are takes its light, and its glow at the screen's edge too. Before you name it, it is empty and its light is small; your pseudo shows on it as you type it, and once taken the light rises to its glow (`--duration-sunrise`), second of the dawn: the corner's path comes down from the mark, the light rises, the question leaves its receipt, then the composer rises, one after another from the top right to the bottom left (`--stagger-dawn`, `--duration-dawn`). Until then it is not there but for its lamp: nothing answers on it, a hover, a click or a menu. Below the root the glow only hints where the root is, waiting at the edge of the screen when the root is off it. The center you look at gets a neutral line, never the warm light: amber says "you", not "here". The rule beside your words and the send button are amber for the same reason, and so is the light of the brand mark, the Sunstone (`layouts/screen/Sunstone.tsx`): a tile cut into six facets, the six things a person cares about, meeting at a sun, the person, its shade side going to the night's `dusk` (HEX-116).
4. **A warm human, a cool agent.** Your words are set in Newsreader, a light serif; the agent's in Geist Mono, violet-marked. What the agent drafts glows violet until you take it. The interface itself speaks in Geist, quietly, so the two voices carry the page. Warning, the colour shadcn would make amber, is an ember orange, so amber keeps one meaning.
5. **Curves as separators.** The conversation is cut along one curve, which also serves as its scrollbar: a star rides it, lighting the stretch you see. Whatever reaches the chat's right side follows the curve, at its own height and one gap short of it, so the conversation's right edge is the curve's and nothing lines up in a column against it: your words and the agent's line by line, each time, each event, each widget's rows, the composer's (`toCurve`, `layouts/screen/state/useAlongCurve.ts`). The layout meets the geometry: the composer's corner sits on the edge of the tile south-west of the center.
6. **Motion tells the hierarchy.** Moving to a neighbour pans; to a child or a parent zooms, the scale changing evenly: the canvas's camera flies as its center changes, the child growing from where it was drawn, its ring with it, the parent and its other children staying around it, past the canvas's edge (`canvas/state/useCamera.ts`); an opened tile's children bloom out of its heart, one after another, and fold back into it. Reaching another System is reaching another star: the one left recedes into the night and the one reached comes close from a point of light (`canvas/layouts/Journey.tsx`), and the corner's mark carries you back to your own sun. Whatever is done, hexframe's mark answers it: the Sunstone at the head of the canvas's corner turns once whole. With reduced motion, everything lands at once.

hexframe is dark, its one theme, the night a sun is seen against. A surface rises toward light, so a tile is always lighter than the ground; the untitled root, which is not there yet, is no surface at all, only its lamp.

### One screen, two sides

hexframe is one screen, with no menu and no page to go to. Everything happens in the chat, where you and the agent act; the canvas carries what you act on, and the way through it. Each side answers the other, and that is most of the art: a tile named in the chat lights up on the canvas, the agent at work marks the tile it is in, and the tile you center on the canvas is where the agent works next. Signed out, it is the same screen: the canvas shows hexframe's help, and the chat asks who you are. On a phone the chat stays in front and the canvas behind it, as the tiles run on behind the conversation on a wide screen; the canvas comes forward when the chat points at it, then gives it back.

## hexframe's screen, in words

hexframe is one screen with two **sides**, and nothing to navigate outside them. The **Chat** shows the Conversation: the user and the agent act there, and whatever a menu would do happens there. The **Canvas** shows the System: it carries what they act on, and the way through it. The **crossings** are where one side answers the other, both ways: a tile named in the chat lights up on the canvas, a tile centered on the canvas is where the agent works next, and, where a tile's body numbers its six, a line pointed at lights its tile and a tile pointed at its line (HEX-122).

A side is made of **components**, a chat component or a canvas component, built from **atoms**, a control that knows nothing of hexframe (a button, a field), inside **layouts**, a shell with slots that decides where things go and how they fold on a phone (the widget's frame, the dialog's). An atom or a layout lives in the side that uses it, and is shared once the other side uses it too. The width picks the layout, a phone showing one side at a time; a finger picks the behaviour, at any width. The tokens on `/design-system` say where each begins (`gallery/Tokens.tsx`).

## Rules

- **A piece's folder is its tier and its side.** A piece's side is the side of the screen it is used on, counting the features that draw each: `features/conversation/` draws the Chat, and `features/system/` the tile widget at its foot, `features/system/`, `features/shipped/` and `features/breadcrumb/` the Canvas; the root's header is on no side, since the one screen retires it, and Keys, Help and signing in are the Chat's widgets since HEX-103 (`hexframe-app-design-system-v1/decisions.md#DEC-5`, in `.run/registers/`). A component belongs to the side it draws; an atom or a layout sits in `<side>/atoms/` or `<side>/layouts/` while one side uses it, and moves to `atoms/` or `layouts/` here once the other side uses it too. A piece whose only users are pages the one screen retires stays where it fits until its last user goes, then goes with it, as `PageHeader` and `DataTable` did (HEX-103). A crossing is no piece and has no folder: each of its ends is its side's, `chat/crossings.tsx` and `canvas/crossings.tsx`, and `crossings.tsx` at `ui/`'s root joins them for the page, as home's route does (the grill of 2026-10-11, Q7). dependency-cruiser holds the order, sides above shared layouts above shared atoms above the seams, and keeps the sides apart (`no-side-importing-the-other`, `no-layouts-importing-up`, `no-atoms-importing-up`, `no-seams-importing-up`), `crossings.tsx` the one file at its root joining them, which nothing in `ui/` imports (`no-ui-root-joining-the-sides`, `no-ui-importing-the-crossings`); `scripts/lint.test.ts` proves each fires. The rule places atoms and layouts, not words: a feature of one side may still read the other side's model where a crossing carries it, as the Conversation tells a navigation in the canvas's `Gesture` (`canvas/view/`); features ignore each other already (`no-feature-importing-another`).
- **Only `ui/` imports the UI libraries**: Radix, TanStack Table, TanStack Form, the Markdown renderer, TanStack Hotkeys and Sonner; `dependency-cruiser.config.ts` says no elsewhere. No raw `<table>` or `<dialog>` outside it, and colour comes from theme tokens, never a palette name or a hex: `eslint.config.ts` says no to both. `scripts/lint.test.ts` proves those rules still fire.
- **A component takes its content as props** (`title`, `entries`, `keys`), not as a family of parts to assemble, so a feature cannot rebuild it differently.
- **Dark, the one theme.** The page is always dark: there is no theme to choose, its toggle gone with the onboarding's iteration, the Sunstone in its place. The tokens and the three fonts are in `src/front/styles.css`, which owns their values: every ink rose to 4.5:1 there (`hexframe-app-design-system-v1/decisions.md#DEC-22`); the light values it still holds, which the dark ones override, wait to go, and until then the tests below keep checking both. A token keeps shadcn's name when it means what shadcn means; the others are named for what they are in hexframe, chiefly `human` (the human's amber), `organization` (an Organization's rose, a star's light as amber is a person's) and `agent` (the agent's violet), the only colours of the interface itself. `brand` is gone, and `warning` is an ember orange, so amber keeps one meaning. What each token is for: its note on `/design-system`, in `gallery/Tokens.tsx`. A risen surface, a menu, a card, the dialog, is the `risen` utility beside them: the popover's light, the lift's shadow and a fine line on top, `--rim` where the line is another. A new token gets a note there, a new colour a value in both themes and a line in `@theme inline` too, all of which `gallery/tokens.test.ts` checks, and a foreground if text sits on it. A colour text is set in reads at 4.5:1 at least on the ground and on a card, in both themes, WCAG's minimum for small text, which `gallery/tokens.test.ts` checks too: `faint` included, the quiet ink stays readable.
- **The list grows by request.** The first one: `Button`, `Input`, `Textarea`, `Field` and `useAppForm`, `Card`, `PageHeader`, `Drawer`, `ConfirmDialog`, `DropdownMenu`, `Tooltip`, `Suggestions` (HEX-84), `Composer` (HEX-99), `Toaster`, `Skeleton`, `EmptyState`, `ErrorState`, `Forbidden`, `DataTable`, `CodeBlock`, and the canvas, `Canvas`. The frames (HEX-96): `Widget`, `Dialog`, `Menu`, which replaced `DropdownMenu`, `HoverCard`, and `Act`, the actions they share. `ConfirmDialog` and `Drawer` stay until their last users move onto the frames, in the builds of M3 and M4; `Tooltip` went with v0's tile (HEX-106). Then the screen's proposal (HEX-95): `Screen` and the canvas's corner, `Rail`, and the conversation's text: `Message`, `Thinking`, `Mention`, `Events`, `DaySplit`, `Proposal`, `Receipt` and `Fold` (HEX-98). Then the tile (HEX-104): `Tile`, in its kinds, states and words, `Slot` and `Ground`, on the canvas since HEX-106, which retired v0's `Tile` and `Frame`, and the `Tooltip` its hover card was drawn with. Then the widgets' (HEX-100): `TileWidget`, where the tile forms, the import and the export move from the `Drawer`, `KeyWidget`, `HelpWidget` and `SignInWidget`, which take the places of the Keys, Help and signing-in pages. Home's chat draws the tile widget (HEX-102), and the key, help and sign-in widgets (HEX-103), which retired `PageHeader` and `DataTable` with the pages they drew. The chat's build (HEX-101) retired `Suggestions`, the composer's list in its place, and `LocaleSwitch`, the language a command now. Then acting on it (HEX-105): `TileMenu`, its menu and its hover card, the frame in small its place submenus show, and `Moving`, what the canvas draws while a tile moves, which home's canvas draws since HEX-107; `DropdownMenu` and `Tooltip` had already gone, and `ConfirmDialog` lost its last user on home, the delete asking in the `Dialog` now, and stays for its gallery alone until it goes. Then the crossings (HEX-108): `Marks`, who touches a tile, drawn on it, and an end on each side of every crossing; `HoverCard` moved to the shared layouts once a tile named in the chat opened it too. Then the screen's iteration: the canvas's ring chooser and `Card`, the centered Tile's card, went, the center standing alone and large until a click opens it, and a canvas only looked at gained the view's lines in its tiles' menus (`looking`); the widgets run the chat's width to its curve. Then onboarding: `Drawer` went with its last user, the Root's edit, now the tile widget's, which asks a new account its pseudo too; a new tile's widget lost its kind and place pickers, its place the one clicked; a widget's body and actions run its whole width, its tools ride the curve, and an `Act` may say what it does by an icon. Then the tutorial (HEX-119): `Journey`, the canvas carried from one System to another, and the `Rail`'s mark a way back. Then sharing (HEX-128): `ShareWidget`, who may see a tile, which its menu's Share opens; and `StarPicker`, a star found by its Title or a link, which the Share widget and an Organization's members will open (HEX-130). Then Organizations (HEX-136): `OrganizationWidget` and `MembersWidget`; and their Groups (HEX-137): `GroupsWidget`, with `GroupWidget`, one group changed, and `GroupPicker`, a group the Share widget shares a tile with.
- **A finger is not a pointer.** Under `pointer-coarse` nothing waits on a hover and a target is at least 44px (`pointer-coarse:` variants): the widget's tools show at once, folded into the one control of its menu; a menu's lines and an action grow to 44px; the hover card never opens, and the menu's header carries what it would have said, opened by the long press. A tap goes into a tile, the double click a finger lacks, and a finger never drags one: it moves it through the menu's frame (`canvas/tile/menu/`, `useFinger`).
