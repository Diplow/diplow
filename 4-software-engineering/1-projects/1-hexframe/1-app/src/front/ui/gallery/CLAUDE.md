# gallery

The frame of `/design-system` ("The page", below): every folder's `gallery.tsx` draws its pieces in `frame.tsx`, which imports none of them, nor any tier, so any tier's gallery may import it. `deck/` is the page itself, as slides: it lays out what the route hands it on the screen's pieces, as the route does, and nothing in a tier imports it.

| Path | Holds |
|---|---|
| `frame.tsx` | `GalleryTier`, which tells a tier's galleries the tier they are drawn under; `ShowingPiece`, the piece the deck shows, of whose galleries inside it the specimens alone are drawn; `GallerySection`, a piece's states, drawn while it is the piece shown, which throws when drawn under another tier than the catalogue's; `tierTitle` and `tierNote`, which the deck tells a chapter with; `GalleryGroup`, a row's heading inside a piece's states; `GalleryState`, one labelled specimen; `GalleryColumn`, the conversation's column a chat specimen stands in, or a phone's; `useSteps`, the clock a timed specimen plays by, a tile's states or a crossing's Turn, each play from its first step, drawn at once |

| `pieces.test.ts` | Each piece's files exist, its "made of" is what they import of the other pieces, through the files that are no piece of their own, pieces sharing a file sharing its imports between them, and the catalogue runs tier by tier, top-down, every tier filled; each crossing joins a piece of the Chat and a piece of the Canvas, none at the other side's end, and names at each end pieces its side's crossings draw, there or inside a piece drawn there |

## The page

`/design-system` (`src/front/routes/design-system.tsx`) shows the design system to anyone, in production too, as a deck of slides, which the chat goes through as home's chat goes through a System: the agent tells each slide, the next one is its last line, ← and → go there, and a `/` in the composer lists every slide. It starts with words on the ground alone, before there is a screen: why hexframe exists, then its six principles. The screen then builds up: the chat first, the canvas still a page of words about it; then the canvas, its first tile alone and large, which a click opens, the chat telling where the reader went in home's words; then the onboarding, a new account's first visit, the screen holding everything back but its question until the reader gives a pseudo, then arriving in turn, played again from the start at will. Then the crossings, each played on the whole screen, its end in the chat, its composer at the chat's foot, its end on the canvas (`ui/crossings.tsx`, `CrossingDemo`). Then a chapter per side and one for what they share, the layouts, the atoms and the seams: the chat lists the chapter's pieces and tells the one shown, the canvas gives way to its states. Then the tokens. The slide, its piece and the canvas's view live in the search params, so a link shows what its sender saw.

Every string is a message, in English and in French; a note says what a piece is for and why, and an open question stays in its ticket or its pull request.

A piece gets its place in three steps:

1. **An entry in `pieces.ts`**, in its tier's place: its name, its tier, the files it is drawn in and its note, a message. Its "made of" is what those files import of the other pieces, through the files that are no piece of their own; `pieces.test.ts` reads it from the imports and fails until the entry says the same. Pieces drawn in one file (`layouts/states.tsx`) share its imports: each names those it draws, and together they name them all. "Used in" is read back from it. A crossing's entry has no files but its two ends, the pieces it joins on each side, which `pieces.test.ts` holds to a piece of the Chat and a piece of the Canvas. Its tier puts it in its chapter (`deck/slides.ts`), which `slides.test.ts` holds to.
2. **A `GallerySection` named after it**, in its folder's `gallery.tsx`, one `GalleryState` per state, on samples that are messages. A feature's piece has its own `gallery.tsx` (`features/conversation/`, `features/breadcrumb/`), and so do the client's channels (`client/gallery.tsx`), since `ui/` imports neither. A crossing is played by `ui/crossings.tsx`, which hands its frame its two ends.
3. **The route draws that gallery in its chapter**, under its tier, every gallery of the chapter drawn and the piece shown alone drawing its states. The route holds the canvas's view in its search params and hands it to the specimens that show it, so a fixture view is a link and a Message's link in the Conversation centers it. The canvas's own specimen sits in the route, and so do the `Screen`'s, which show the breadcrumb and Help, which `ui/` can't import, each holding its own view and side in front: signed in on the fixture System, signed out on Help itself, read as home reads it signed out. A file beside the route would need a name the router skips, and a name starting with `-` is one transcrypt encrypts (`.gitattributes`). `GallerySection` throws when drawn under another tier than the catalogue's.

The canvas and the Conversation run on fixtures (`canvas/fixtures.ts`, `features/conversation/fixtures.ts`), the user's own words, not translated. The chat's pieces run on samples that are messages (`chat/samples.ts`), so they read in the page's language, and so does the canvas's first tile (`deck/tour.tsx`). The help widget's states read Help itself, as the signed-out `Screen` does, in the route, which hands it to `HelpWidgetGallery`; the tile widget's samples are the chat's sample tiles, their words messages too (`chat/widgets/tile/fixtures.ts`). The channels show on domains' refusals and an `Unexpected`, with no call made.
